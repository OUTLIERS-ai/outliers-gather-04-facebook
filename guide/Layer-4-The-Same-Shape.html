Layer 4 · The Same Shape

The same four parts, on Facebook

Everything so far has been LinkedIn. This layer hands you a working Facebook version, so you can read the two side by side and see how little had to change.

What the last layer left you with

You know how the first two layers were built, and you have read one page on a site of your own. Building all of it again from nothing, for a second website, still sounds like a great deal of work.

What this layer builds

A Facebook engine, with the same four parts you already have:

  • the doorman, here called facebook_limits.py
  • a second browser with its own folder, separate from yours
  • a sign-in you do by hand, once, with no time limit
  • the rule that a job reads a page before it acts

It arrives with its list of search words empty, and it will refuse to run until you fill it in. Which groups your people are in is the one question nobody can answer for you, and an empty list is what stops you skipping it.

Layer 4 · How it works

How it works

What moved across untouched, what changed, and the one number that is lower.

What moved across untouched

All four parts. The doorman and its six questions. One browser with its own folder. The sign-in you do by hand. The rule that a job reads a page before it acts. None of those four is about LinkedIn or about Facebook. They are about driving a browser on an account you cannot replace, which is the same problem on every website there is.

What changed

The words it searches for, and the descriptions it uses to find things on a page. That is the whole of it. A second engine is the same system with a different list of words and a different set of page descriptions.

The number that is lower, and why

Joining groups is capped at five a day, the lowest number anywhere in this set. On Facebook, losing the account loses every group you were in with it. The limit comes from the size of the mistake, not from how much work you want done, so every website needs its own answer, not a number copied across from this one.

Sites that do not need your account

Not every website needs you signed in. Some can be read while signed out, which means an ordinary private window (Incognito in Chrome, Private in Safari) that forgets everything when you close it, no sign-in, and almost no risk. Check whether you are in that situation before assuming you are not, because it is a much easier job.

What has not been tested

The design is proven. The engine this came from has run against Facebook for real and joined real groups under these limits. This copy has not. The order to prove it in is on the next page, and running it is the first job.

Layer 4 · Prove it first

Prove it, in this order

Four steps, and this is the first job, not an optional check. Stop at the first one that surprises you.

All four are typed in the _engine folder inside your CRM. On a Mac, type python3 wherever this says python.

python facebook.py login

1. A browser window opens on Facebook. Sign in as yourself and keep going until you reach your normal feed, then come back and press Enter. No time limit.

python facebook.py probe

2. It opens your Facebook home page, saves a picture of it and a written description of what is on it, and prints where it put both. It collects nobody.

python facebook.py find-groups --query "your words here"

3. This should print group names. If it does, the reading half works.

4. Open the list it wrote before you join anything. Which groups are worth being in is your decision and the job will not make it for you.

If step 3 prints nothing

You cannot yet tell whether the search genuinely found no groups, or whether Facebook has moved the page under it. Those look identical from where you are sitting. Step 2 is what separates them: the description it saved tells you what the page is made of now, and whether the parts this engine looks for are still there.

Build it

Three lines, typed into a terminal. The third asks you 6 questions, writes your answers into your own settings file, and stops.
git clone https://github.com/OUTLIERS-ai/outliers-gather-04-facebook
cd outliers-gather-04-facebook
python install.py

This stands on its own and does not need the other layers, though it expects the same records system underneath. Nothing reaches the outside world during installation.

What it asks you, and what it offers

QuestionWhat it changes

Layer 4 · Finishing

Once it is proven

Day to day, it is two commands.

Everything is typed in the _engine folder inside your CRM.

python facebook.py find-groups
python facebook.py join --commit

The first sweeps every word in your list and writes a ranked list of groups. The second asks to join the ones you put in the queue, one at a time, up to five a day.

Between those two there is a step that is yours: opening the list and deciding which groups are worth being in. Nothing joins anything you have not put in the queue by hand.

If you already had something like this

Put every website you use in order of the worst outcome of a mistake. Compare that order to your limits. Most people's limits are in a different order.

What this layer leaves unsolved

Two websites, both working, and you type every command yourself. Nothing happens unless you are sitting there, so it happens on the days you remember and not on the days you are busy.
